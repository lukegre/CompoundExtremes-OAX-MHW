2×
summer vs winter

Compound extremes occur nearly twice as often in summer. Their latitude range expands poleward in summer and contracts toward the tropics in winter.

El Niño – La Niña connection

Compound area peaks at the end of — or just after — El Niño events, often during the following La Niña.

Why do they happen?

A marine heatwave warms the surface ocean, which shifts seawater chemistry toward higher [H⁺] — making it more acidic.

Thermal push
Warming raises [H⁺]
vs
DIC buffer
Falling DIC lowers [H⁺]
A compound extreme forms when the DIC buffer fails to offset the warming-driven rise in [H⁺].

Most common in permanently stratified, low- to mid-latitude oceans, where the seasonal [H⁺] cycle is governed by temperature.
